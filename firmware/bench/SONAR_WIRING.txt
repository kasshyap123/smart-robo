Microphone is set aside at the owner's request; it is not confirmed dead.
The owner confirmed five 1 kohm resistors are available/identified.

Unplug USB before wiring. Disconnect ALL six mic connections and set the mic
aside. Leave batteries, motors, driver, amplifier, buzzer and servos disconnected.
Use ONE HC-SR04 at a time. Use its printed VCC/TRIG/ECHO/GND labels.

OLED (unchanged): VCC=3V3, GND=GND, SDA=GPIO21, SCL=GPIO22.
HC-SR04: VCC=ESP32 pin labelled 5V/VIN while powered by USB; GND=common GND;
TRIG=GPIO13. Its ECHO goes through the five-resistor divider below to GPIO35.
Keep HC-SR04 5V supply separate from the OLED 3.3V rail. Do not feed 5V into 3V3.
If the labelled VIN pin has no near-5V output under USB, stop and identify the
board's actual 5V supply connection rather than guessing another pin.

ALL FIVE RESISTORS ARE 1 kohm. Each resistor joins end-to-end in SERIES:

HC ECHO -- R1 -- R2 -- JUNCTION -- R3 -- R4 -- R5 -- common GND
                          |
                       GPIO35

R1+R2 = 2 kohm between HC ECHO and junction.
R3+R4+R5 = 3 kohm between junction and GND.
GPIO35 connects ONLY to the junction, not directly to HC ECHO.
The junction is one shared breadboard five-hole group; each intermediate resistor
connection uses a different shared group. Each resistor must span two DIFFERENT
groups, so it isn't bypassed by a breadboard's internal metal clip.

At 5V Echo HIGH, junction is nominally 5 x 3/(2+3) = 3.0V.
For 4.75-5.25V Echo and 5% resistors, theoretical HIGH range is 2.735-3.275V.
This leaves margin above the ESP32 logic-high threshold and below 3.3V.
Do not power until the Echo divider is assembled correctly. A soldered divider
isn't required for this breadboard test.
